## UK Visa Photo Specifications 2026

For the online UK visa application (which covers Standard Visitor, Student, Work, and Family visas):

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| Physical Size | **35 mm × 45 mm** (or equivalent digital crop) |
| File Format | **JPEG** |
| File Size | **Under 4 MB** (for digital upload via UKVCAS app) |
| Dimensions | Minimum **600 × 750 pixels** |
| Background | **Plain cream or off-white** (note: UK allows cream, not just pure white) |
| Photo Age | Taken within the last **6 months** |
| Print | If printed, on photo-quality paper |

**Important:** Unlike US visa requirements (which mandate pure white backgrounds), the UK specifies **cream or off-white** as the background colour. A pure white background is also acceptable.

## Composition Requirements

### Head and Face
- Look straight at the camera with your face fully in frame
- Head should fill between **50% and 70%** of the frame height
- Both eyes open, clearly visible
- Mouth closed, neutral expression

### Glasses
Following ICAO 2024 updates, the UK now **recommends against wearing glasses** in visa photos and states they may be rejected. This aligns with the trend across Schengen and US visa requirements. If you wear glasses, remove them for the photo.

### Background
The background should be plain cream or off-white. No patterns, gradients, or shadows. The UK's slight preference for cream over pure white is a distinctive requirement — though in practice, UKVCAS digital upload accepts both.

### Lighting
Even, diffuse lighting on the face with no shadows. Natural window light (not direct sunlight) is ideal for home photos.

### Clothing
Everyday clothing that contrasts with the background. No uniforms (unless worn daily for religious reasons). Head coverings only for documented religious reasons, with the full face visible.

## The Digital Upload Process for UK Visa

UK visa applicants from India submit applications through the UKVCAS (UK Visa and Citizenship Application Services) system, currently operated by TLScontact or VFS Global depending on your region. The process:

1. Submit your online visa application on gov.uk
2. Book a biometric appointment at a VFS/TLScontact centre
3. Upload your photograph digitally through the UKVCAS app or portal
4. Bring two printed 45×35mm photos to your appointment (as backup)

The digital upload accepts files up to 4MB, which is generous enough that most smartphone photos pass without compression. However, you still need to ensure the composition meets all visual requirements.

## Differences Between UK, US, and Schengen Photo Requirements

| Requirement | UK | US (DS-160) | Schengen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical Size | 35×45mm | 51×51mm (2×2") | 35×45mm |
| Background | Cream/off-white | Pure white | Pure white |
| Digital Size Limit | 4 MB | 240 KB | 300 KB |
| Head Coverage | 50–70% of height | 50–69% of height | 70–80% of height |
| Glasses | Not recommended | Prohibited | Prohibited |

The UK has the most permissive file size limit (4MB vs 240KB for US), a distinctive cream background preference, and is slightly less strict on the head coverage range.

## Common UK Visa Photo Rejection Reasons

1. **Old photo** — taken more than 6 months before application
2. **Wearing glasses** — increasingly rejected following ICAO 2024 standards
3. **Background too dark or coloured** — grey, blue, or patterned walls are not acceptable
4. **Shadow on face or background** — common with home photos taken near walls
5. **Low resolution** — digital photos under 600×750 pixels are rejected
6. **File wrong format** — using PNG, BMP, or HEIC instead of JPEG

## How to Take a Good UK Visa Photo at Home

The UK's cream background requirement makes it slightly easier than the pure white required for US/Schengen — any neutral light-coloured wall will work. Follow these steps:

1. Stand against a plain cream or light-coloured wall with adequate indoor light
2. Place a single bright lamp or face a large window to reduce shadows
3. Have someone take the photo at eye level, not from above (selfie) or below
4. Frame the shot so your face takes up 50–70% of the vertical space
5. Take multiple shots and select the sharpest one
